## section: history

Carina takes its name from an 1850s estate on Creek Road belonging to Ebenezer Thorne, named for a daughter of the family, Kate Carina. The district was the main settlement centre of the Shire of Belmont from 1888, the post office opened in 1889, and the same year the Stanley Street Extended Estate put 275 allotments under the hammer in a single on-site auction.

The trams came early and left their mark. The Belmont Shire's own tramway ran through Carina from 1912 to 1926, and when Brisbane's electric tram was extended along Old Cleveland Road in 1948, the track was laid in mass concrete, one of the first uses of that construction in the city. Roughly 625 metres of it still sits in the Old Cleveland Road median between Camp Hill and Carina, on the Queensland Heritage Register since 2000.

Most of the suburb's streets went up after the war, through the Housing Commission era of the 1950s and 60s, with the institutions following: St Martin's Catholic primary in 1954, Mayfield State School in 1956, San Sisto College in 1961. One house predates all of it. Flint's Cottage at 40 Lunga Street, built in 1892, is the only surviving 19th-century residence in Carina and sits on the city's local heritage list.

A quirk buyers trip on: Carina State School, opened in 1917, has been physically in Carindale since a 1980 boundary change, while keeping its original name. The state primary actually inside Carina is Mayfield.

For a seller, the story is a suburb that has been a centre of its district since the 1880s, kept its full-sized post-war blocks, and still carries its history in the median strip of its main road.

## section: transport

Carina does not have its own railway station. The closest stations are Cannon Hill and Coorparoo on the Cleveland line, both a drive for most Carina addresses. Live timetables at translink.com.au/plan-your-journey.

The bus spine is Old Cleveland Road, where the 200 runs to Queen Street, the 204 to Fortitude Valley and the 222 to Roma Street, all confirmed at the Carina stops. Creek Road adds the north-south connection, and the Carindale bus station interchange sits just east of the suburb for cross-town routes.

## section: pockets

Carina breaks into three pockets buyers consider distinctly.

The catchment streets toward Whites Hill and the Cavendish Road corridor are the premium pocket for the family-home market. School access drives buyer interest, and homes here are bought by families who have done the boundary homework before they inspect.

The Pacific Golf Club area includes established residential streets with character home stock and a settled, held-long feel. Homes trade infrequently in this pocket relative to the rest of the suburb.

The Old Cleveland Road corridor sits closer to the arterial and offers the suburb's most accessible entry point, with townhouse development mixed among the original homes.

## section: lifestyle

The Carina Leagues Club on Creek Road is one of the most underrated community assets in Brisbane's east. Families use it for sport, events and dining, and it anchors the settled, community-oriented character the suburb is known for. The Clem Jones Centre on Zahel Street, founded by the former Lord Mayor it is named for, adds sporting facilities most suburbs cannot match: four pools including an indoor pool, a gym, a basketball stadium, netball, squash and tennis courts, and resident clubs across half a dozen sports. The Carina Tigers have played rugby league at Leo Williams Oval on the Creek and Stanley Road corner since 1958, and AC Carina runs its football from Fursden Road.

A small cafe run works Old Cleveland Road through the suburb, and Westfield Carindale is a short drive for big-format retail and dining. Camp Hill Marketplace and the Martha Street cafes handle village dining within a 5 to 10 minute drive.

For open space, thirteen council parks sit inside the suburb, including Belmont Recreation Reserve on Old Cleveland Road and two fenced dog off-leash areas. A slice of Minnippi Parklands falls inside Carina's northern edge, the Bulimba Creek bikeway runs the same corridor, and Whites Hill Reserve sits just west in Camp Hill and Carina Heights.

The lifestyle here is family-suburb east Brisbane: catchment, parks, club and sport infrastructure, and an accessible entry point relative to the inner-east suburbs to the west.

## section: buyer profile

Three buyer types drive the Carina market.

The largest is family upgraders targeting the school catchments and the family-suburb feel. Many arrive as underbidders from Camp Hill and Coorparoo campaigns, already educated on value and ready to act.

The second group is buyers who want land. Carina has retained a significant share of its original full-sized allotments, and buyers planning pools, sheds or extensions can still find the space here without moving to the outer ring.

The third group is renovators. The post-war stock attracts buyers looking for well-located projects, and the spread between original and fully renovated homes gives them room to add value.

## section: selling approach

Selling in Carina starts with the catchment question: identify whether the property sits inside the sought-after school boundaries and build the campaign accordingly.

A catchment-access property runs the family campaign, with school access and the suburb's community infrastructure leading the story. These campaigns draw the deepest buyer pools and multiple offers are the normal outcome for well-presented homes.

An out-of-catchment property runs the land and value campaign: block size, proximity to the Leagues Club precinct and Westfield Carindale, and the gap to Camp Hill pricing as the central proposition.

On method, both auction and private treaty work depending on the property and the buyer pool. The spillover demand from the west means offer deadlines are effective at converting educated underbidders into committed buyers.
