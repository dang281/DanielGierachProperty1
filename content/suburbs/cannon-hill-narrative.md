## section: history

Cannon Hill House went up in 1867 for the Weedon family, and how it got its name is genuinely disputed. One account has Richard Weedon naming it after a botanical reserve near a Birmingham cannon works; another has Thornhill Weedon naming it for two fallen trees that resembled a cannon. Both are on the record and neither is settled, which is very Cannon Hill. What is certain is that when the estate was subdivided after the railway arrived in 1889, the new streets took artillery names, and Shrapnel, Gatling and Grenade Roads are still on the map. The house itself burned down in 1926.

The station opened in 1910 and by 1913 was the first in Queensland fitted with boom gates, because that year Swift's built its huge riverside meatworks on the Murarrie boundary and the rail siding came with it. Then came the suburb's defining institution: the Cannon Hill Saleyards, opened by the Premier in November 1931 on 60 hectares designed by Richard Gailey Jr. Over sixty years, more than 47 million head of livestock from as far as the Northern Territory passed through, on sealed yards that were ahead of their time for disease control. The saleyards closed in 1991 and the footprint became housing and an office park.

The heritage survives in the everyday. The 1915 Cannon Hill State School building joined the Queensland Heritage Register in 2015, and the city's local list holds the 1910 station, the 1923 School of Arts on Wynnum Road, the 1921 St Oliver Plunkett church, and even a 1948 Housing Commission duplex on Moncrief Road from the post-war build-out.

For a seller, the point is that Cannon Hill has been working infrastructure for over a century, and the same connectedness that moved 47 million cattle now moves commuters: train, interchange, Gateway. Buyers pay for that practicality.

## section: transport

Cannon Hill has its own railway station on the Cleveland line, in service since 1910. Most Cannon Hill streets are within a 5 to 15 minute walk of the station, which is one of the practical reasons the suburb consistently attracts transport-focused buyers. Off-peak train travel to the city runs around 20 minutes. Live timetables at translink.com.au/plan-your-journey.

The Cannon Hill bus interchange at the Wynnum and Creek Road corner, beside the Kmart Plaza, carries the 220 CityXpress to the Valley and the 215 between Carindale and the city, with other routes through the corridor. Routes change with service updates, so the journey planner is the reliable source for any specific street.

By car, the Gateway Motorway is a short, direct run east along Wynnum Road to the Tingalpa interchange, which handles airport and M1 routes. The Story Bridge puts the CBD around 10 to 14 minutes away off-peak.

For active transport, the Bulimba Creek bikeway runs through the suburb toward Minnippi and Carindale. Most streets have walking access to the Kmart Plaza precinct.

## section: schools

Cannon Hill State School on Wynnum Road is the local state primary, teaching since 1915 in a building that is now state heritage-listed. Catchment boundaries vary by street and should be confirmed through the Queensland Government's catchment lookup at [qgso.qld.gov.au/maps/edmap](https://www.qgso.qld.gov.au/maps/edmap).

For secondary state schooling there is no government high school inside the suburb. Balmoral State High School and Whites Hill State College are the commonly cited options, and which applies genuinely varies by address, so the lookup matters here more than in most suburbs.

For private schooling, Cannon Hill holds two of its own: Cannon Hill Anglican College on the Junction and Krupp Road corner, and St Oliver Plunkett Catholic primary on Beauvardia Street, teaching since 1947. Lourdes Hill in Hawthorne and the East Brisbane schools are a short drive.

## section: pockets

Cannon Hill breaks into three pockets that buyers consider distinctly.

The station-adjacent pocket is built around walking access to Cannon Hill station. Mix of character homes and post-war stock, the most contested pocket for transport-focused buyers, and the segment of the suburb most closely tied to the train commute.

The family streets sit further from the arterials. Quieter residential character, established family-home stock, larger blocks, and the suburb's traditional character home market. This is the part of Cannon Hill that matches what most buyers picture when they imagine an inner-east family suburb.

The Wynnum Road corridor sits closer to the arterial. Convenient for transport but trades on a different value proposition than the quieter family streets. Properties here can offer a more accessible entry point into the suburb.

When buyers ask which Cannon Hill pocket to target, the answer depends on whether they prioritise transport, residential quiet, or value.

## section: lifestyle

Cannon Hill runs two separate retail centres a kilometre apart, which surprises people who lump them together. The Kmart Plaza at the Creek and Wynnum Road corner carries Kmart, Coles and about fifty specialty stores, while Cannon Central at 1145 Wynnum Road answers with ALDI, Woolworths and TK Maxx. The Southgate Avenue precinct adds a third cluster of cafes and services on the business-park side.

The newest asset is Minnippi Golf and Range on Creek Road, the public course Brisbane City Council opened in 2023, with the Cooee cafe on site. The Cannon Hill Community Sports Club, the old bowls club running since 1966 on Princess Street, and the Cannon Hill Stars junior rugby league on Wynnum Road carry the club life.

For weekend dining, the Bulimba and Hawthorne village strips are within seven to ten minutes by car for the broader inner-east cafe and restaurant range.

For open space, eleven council parks sit inside the suburb, headlined by the Cannon Hill Bushland Reserve behind the plaza, ten hectares of bushcare-tended bushland on Bulimba Creek. The Bulimba Creek bikeway threads the suburb toward the wider Minnippi Parklands next door in Tingalpa and Carina. One honest note: there is no dog off-leash area inside Cannon Hill itself, the nearest are in Camp Hill and Tingalpa.

The lifestyle here is practical inner-east. Train, retail, golf, and residential character bundled in one suburb.

## section: buyer profile

Four buyer types drive the Cannon Hill market.

The largest group is inner-east upgraders. Buyers moving up from Tingalpa, Murarrie, or further east who want the train station and the K-Mart Plaza precinct on the doorstep. They arrive with budget and local knowledge to recognise which streets work for them.

The second group is established families. Parents targeting the Cannon Hill catchment corridor and the family-suburb feel of the quieter residential streets. The school catchment overlap is the central driver for this segment.

The third group is interstate relocators, particularly from Sydney and Melbourne, drawn by the airport proximity and the inner-east transport convenience. The Gateway Motorway access supports frequent travel routines.

The fourth group is investors. Train station and arterial proximity supports rental demand, and Cannon Hill consistently produces yield numbers that work for investor buyers buying on the numbers.

A campaign here speaks to all four. The pocket and stock type determine which segment leads.

## section: selling approach

Selling in Cannon Hill comes down to identifying which buyer group the specific property speaks to most strongly and building the campaign around that segment.

A station-adjacent property runs the transport-focused campaign. The walk to the station is the asset, the daily-commute proposition is the story, and the buyer profile leans heavily toward upgraders and professionals.

A family-street property runs the family-home campaign. School catchment access, residential quiet, and block size lead the marketing. Photography focuses on the home and the street character rather than the transport story.

A Wynnum Road corridor property runs the value campaign. The proposition is honest about what the property is and what it is not, and attracts buyers looking for accessible entry into the suburb rather than the premium pocket.

On method, both auction and private treaty work in Cannon Hill. Station-adjacent and family-street properties with broad appeal often suit auction. Wynnum Road corridor and investor-grade properties can suit private treaty depending on buyer pool depth.

## section: comparable suburbs

Buyers considering Cannon Hill typically also look at Morningside, Murarrie, Tingalpa, and Norman Park.

[Morningside](/suburbs/morningside) is the natural neighbour with similar train access and a stronger village strip on Wynnum Road. Often considered together and the choice frequently comes down to which catchment and which pocket the buyer prioritises.

[Murarrie](/suburbs/murarrie) is the value-east alternative. Lower entry point, similar transport access for parts of the suburb, and the natural next look for buyers who start their search in Cannon Hill.

[Tingalpa](/suburbs/tingalpa) is east of Cannon Hill with a similar value proposition at a similar price level. Different residential character, similar transport access for parts of the suburb.

[Norman Park](/suburbs/norman-park) is the inner alternative for buyers who want character stock and topography. Different lifestyle, different price profile, often considered together with Cannon Hill by buyers searching the inner east broadly.
